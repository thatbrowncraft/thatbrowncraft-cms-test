The old wall clock clicked past eleven.

A quiet rhythm filled the office of **Chandani & Associates**, one of Ahmedabad's well respected chartered accountancy firms. The constant tapping of keyboards, printers humming in the background and calculators clicking every few seconds had become background music for everyone working there.

Everyone...

Except one girl.

The AC in the small accounting firm was doing its absolute best impression of a functioning appliance which is to say, not much. Devanshi Patel had her hair tied back like a warrior's messy bun, three ledgers open in front of her, and a calculator she trusted more than most people.

"Twenty-three lakh eighty-six thousand..."

"minus GST adjustment..."

"carry forward..."

"...no, that's wrong."

Devanshi frowned slightly before crossing out a figure with her blue pen. She wasn't the type to leave calculations halfway. If a number didn't make sense, she wouldn't sleep until it did.

Her desk was the neatest in the department despite being covered with files, sticky notes and handwritten flowcharts only she could understand.

She was that girl in the office. The one who could catch a misplaced decimal from three desks away. Junior accountant on paper, low-key menace in practice.

She tucked a loose strand of hair behind her ear and continued typing.

"Devanshi."

She looked up. The office assistant stood beside her, "Sir is calling you."

She blinked. "Sir, I'm literally mid-reconciliation-"

"Now means now."

She sighed, capped her pen like she was sheathing a sword, and walked in. She nodded, "Coming."

Saving her spreadsheet, she picked up her notebook and walked towards the conference room, mentally replaying every assignment she'd worked on over the past week.

_Did she miss anything?

Did a client complain?

Was another audit assigned?_

She pushed open the conference room door, "May I come in, sir?"

"Come, Devanshi." Her boss, Abhinav Chandani, gestured toward the chair, "Please."

The moment she stepped into the meeting room, the air felt different. Heavier. Like the room itself had straightened its spine.

Only after stepping inside did she notice the second man.

He looked to be in his early fifties. Perfectly ironed cream shirt, slight silver hair combed back, the kind of calm that didn't need to raise its voice to be obeyed. Everyone else in the room looked like they were trying not to breathe too loud.

No unnecessary display of wealth. Yet...

The room itself somehow revolved around him. There was a calm authority about him that didn't need announcing.

"Devanshi," her boss said, practically glowing, "meet Mr. Harshvardhan Panchal. Chairman of Panchal Group of Industries."

Devanshi blinked, "The Harshvardhan Panchal?"

The man smiled warmly, "I hope there isn't another one."

She immediately stood straighter.

Anyone in Gujarat's business circle knew that name.

**Chairman of Panchal Group of Industries.**

A Gujarati businessman whose empire stretched across infrastructure, finance, pharmaceuticals, manufacturing, software, textiles, renewable energy and countless startups.

The Harshvardhan Panchal. The man whose name was basically a synonym for "empire" in half the country and apparently a few pincodes abroad too.

The newspapers called him a visionary. Young entrepreneurs called him an investor. Employees called him the best boss they'd ever worked for. Business magazines simply called him...

"The Builder."

Not because he built companies. Because he built people. 

Abhinav couldn't help smiling, "Sir has probably created more employment than half the industrialists combined."

Harshvardhan chuckled, "Abhinav beta, you're exaggerating."

"No, I am not sir."

Both men laughed.

Her boss went on and on — his vision, his ventures, how many startups he'd built from scratch, how many branches he'd planted like flags across the map. Devanshi nodded politely, but internally she was thinking, _okay but why is a man this big sitting in our tiny, leaking-AC office?_

Her colleague, Sagar, leaned over and whispered, smiling, "Our boss, Abhinav sir... he's the son of industrialist Veerchand Chandani. Family friends with the Panchals."

_Ohh. Mystery solved._ 

Devanshi nodded and went back to pretending she wasn't curious anymore.

She had heard stories. How Panchal Group never delayed salaries. How employees stayed there for decades. How Harshvardhan remembered everyone's names despite thousands working under him. How even CEOs respected him.

She had secretly dreamed of working there someday. But dreams...

...were dreams.

Then the conversation shifted to investment files, year-end numbers, a mismatch nobody in the room could explain. Watching grown men in expensive suits sweat over a spreadsheet was, frankly, a little bit funny.

"So, Devanshi," Abhinav said, "We're facing a small issue."

Harshvardhan slid a thick file towards her, "Investment reconciliation."

She opened it. And there were hundreds of pages with so many numbers, flowcharts, capital allocation, subsidiary reports, International transfers, Dividend statements and more. It wasn't a junior accountant's task.

She looked up, "Sir...?"

Harshvardhan smiled, "Everyone has checked it."

Abhinav sighed, "Three times. The totals aren't matching."

"Difference?" She asked leaning on table.

"Thirty-two lakh, forty-seven thousand, eight hundred and sixty." Devanshi frowned. That wasn't a rounding error. That was a misplaced transaction.

Or...

Something worse.

She looked through the summaries. Then the ledger. Then supporting vouchers. Then cash flow statements.

Everyone watched silently. Not a single unnecessary movement was there. Not a single dramatic expression. Only concentration.

Five minutes passed.

Ten.

Fifteen.

She wasn't supposed to look. She definitely wasn't supposed to lean over and start cross-checking numbers from the file in Harshvardhan's own hands. But her fingers were already moving before her brain caught up.

She suddenly looked up, "Sir..."

"Hm?"

"May I see the previous quarter's file too?" She pointed toward the blue folder resting beside Harshvardhan. The room went silent. Her boss looked like he wanted to disappear into his chair.

But Harshvardhan, completely unbothered... slid the file across the table himself. "Of course, beta."

Abhinav raised an eyebrow, "That isn't related."

"It is." She dove in. Column by column, entry by entry, that sharp accountant's brain of hers lighting up every discrepancy like a Diwali string of lights. 

Harshvardhan quietly observed.

_Interesting_.

She wasn't searching randomly. She was eliminating possibilities. Like solving a puzzle.

Twenty minutes later, she looked up and closed both files, "I found it."

Abhinav almost leaned forward, "What? Already?"

She placed both files side by side, "The investment totals are correct."

Both men stared, "What?"

 She nodded, "The mismatch isn't in investments. There's a rerouted expenditure under a shell vendor code... here, and here." She tapped the page twice. "It's not fraud, just terrible flowchart mapping. But it's why your numbers don't add up."

Silence.

She pointed at a chart, "This subsidiary shifted vendor payments from Operational Expenditure to Capital Improvement."

Another page.

"This branch booked depreciation before asset capitalization."

Another.

"This international transfer is converted using last quarter's exchange rate."

She looked at Harshvardhan, "And this..."

She tapped one figure, "...was entered twice because both departments assumed the other hadn't processed it."

Abhinav quickly grabbed his calculator.

Harshvardhan opened another document.

Click.

Click.

Click.

Thirty seconds later...

Abhinav slowly looked up, "...Zero." 

Everything matched. The conference room fell silent.

Harshvardhan looked at Devanshi for a long moment. Then...

He smiled, "You know..." He folded his glasses.

"...smart people are dangerous."

Devanshi blinked, "I'm sorry?"

"They don't stop at the answer." He pointed toward the files, "They ask whether the question itself is wrong."

She smiled awkwardly, "I just followed the money, sir."

Harshvardhan laughed, "I like that answer."

Abhinav shook his head in disbelief, "I told you she's different."

"No." Harshvardhan corrected gently. "You undersold her, Abhinav." Devanshi lowered her eyes, embarrassed. 

Then Harshvardhan Panchal laughed — a warm, genuine laugh and looked at her like a man who'd just found something rare, "What is your name again?"

"Devanshi, sir."

"How long have you worked here?"

"Almost one year, sir."

"And before this?"

"Fresh graduate."

"Hm." He nodded thoughtfully.

"Devanshi." He said it slowly, like he was filing it away permanently. Then asked the question that completely stunned her, "Would you like to work at Panchal Group?"

Her head snapped up, "...Sir?"

"I mean it."

Abhinav smiled knowingly, "I knew this was coming."

Devanshi looked between both men. Her dream company. The company every commerce student wished to enter. The company people prepared years for. Offering...

_...her?_

"I..." She hesitated, "I can't leave like this."

Harshvardhan looked surprised, "You don't want to?"

"I do." She smiled honestly. "Very much."

"Then?"

She'd said no, at first. Guilt gnawing at her, Abhinav had given her, her first job, her first chance. She looked toward Abhinav, "He trusted me when nobody knew my name. I don't want to betray that trust."

The room became quiet again.

Harshvardhan looked at Abhinav. Abhinav simply smiled, "There. I told you. She's exactly the kind of employee you'd want, Harsh uncle."

He turned toward Devanshi, "You aren't betraying me, Ms. Patel. You're making me proud."

Her eyes softened, "But sir..."

But Abhinav himself pushed her forward, "Devanshi, this is the Panchal Group we're talking about. This is not an opportunity, this is destiny knocking. You'll regret saying no. Go for it. Or else I'll terminate your contract with my company."

She laughed nervously, "I'll only join after clearing every interview. No recommendations. No shortcuts. If I deserve Panchal Group..."

"...I'll earn it."

Harshvardhan stood. Walked around the table. And extended his hand, "Deal." 

She shook it.

His smile widened, "Welcome."

"...Well." she hesitated. 

He chuckled, "Almost welcome."

Devanshi laughed for the first time since entering the room. She had no idea...

...that accepting this opportunity would also introduce her to the single most infuriating man she'd ever meet. A man named...

**Yug Panchal.**

She cleared it easily, obviously, and walked into Panchal Group of Industries as _Accounting & Operations Executive,_ heart pounding with equal parts terror and thrill.

Panchal Group wasn't just a company. It was a legacy... Ahmedabad's pride, a name whispered with respect across the country. And she was going to be a part of it.

The towering glass façade of **Panchal Group of Industries** Headquarters shimmered under the Ahmedabad sun. The company logo, engraved in brushed steel, reflected years of reputation.

People didn't just work here. They built careers here.

Devanshi stood outside the revolving doors, appointment letter clutched in one hand. She took a slow breath,"_Okay... don't embarrass yourself on Day One."_

The receptionist greeted her with a smile, "Good morning, ma'am. Chairman is expecting you."

She blinked, "_The Chairman... expecting me?"_

Five minutes later, she stood outside the Chairman's cabin and knocked softly.

"Come in."

The familiar warm voice instantly eased her nervousness. Harshvardhan looked up from his paperwork and smiled, "There she is. You cleared every round."

"I told you I would only join if I deserved it, sir." Devanshi shrugged playfully maintaining her professionalism. 

"You did." He stood. "And now..." He straightened his coat dramatically, "...time to meet the chaos I proudly call my sons."

Devanshi laughed, "I've heard they're brilliant."

"They are." He sighed dramatically, "Unfortunately."

She couldn't help laughing again. Harshvardhan personally introduced her to his sons.

The first cabin belonged to the Managing Director. Harshvardhan pushed the door open without knocking. Inside, a tall man in a charcoal suit was reviewing files while speaking to someone over Bluetooth.

"...No. Delay the Singapore shipment by forty-eight hours." He looked up. His serious expression softened instantly, "Dad."

"Busy?"

"Not really." He ended the call immediately.

Harshvardhan gestured, "Meet Devanshi Patel. Our newest Accounting & Operations Executive."

The man extended his hand, "Raghav Panchal."

His handshake was firm, so professional, "I've gone through your interview assessments."

He smiled slightly, "Highest analytical score in the last three recruitment cycles."

Devanshi looked surprised, "You saw those?"

"I see every senior hiring." He shrugged, "I like knowing who's joining my company."

His company. Not _my father's._

That one sentence told her enough. He had earned that chair. Not inherited it.

Raghav gave her a brief, assessing nod. "Dad doesn't recruit on a whim. Impress me next, Ms. Patel. Welcome to Panchal Group. I hope you enjoy working here."

"I already think I will."

"I'll hold you to that." 

The next cabin looked completely different. With Bookshelves full of Law journals. Legal documents stacked with frightening perfection.

A man wearing spectacles looked up. Without greeting anyone. He continued highlighting a page. Only after finishing the paragraph did he remove his glasses.

"Dad."

Harshvardhan smiled knowingly, "Nisarg. This is Devanshi."

He stood. Unlike Raghav, his smile barely reached his lips, very measured and observant.

"Nisarg Panchal. Corporate legal advisor."

She shook his hand.

"I've heard you're excellent with numbers."

"I've heard you're impossible to argue against."

A tiny smirk appeared, "You've done your homework."

"I prefer being prepared."

"So do I."

Harshvardhan chuckled, "Good. At least neither of you will bore each other."

Nisarg was leaner, quieter, with the calm precision of a man who read contracts for fun. He smiled politely. "Welcome aboard."

Harshvardhan stopped outside another cabin. Instead of opening it, he sighed, "...Prepare yourself."

Devanshi frowned, "What happened?"

"He happened."

Before she could ask, the door burst open.

A man walked out while simultaneously typing on his laptop, talking through wireless earbuds and balancing a coffee mug, "No, no, no."

"If the firewall is crashing after the API update, don't restart the server. Who's the genius that thought that was a good idea?"

He finally looked up, "Oh."

"Dad." He removed one earbud, "What brings you here?"

Harshvardhan pointed, "This is Devanshi Patel. Our new Accounting & Operations Executive."

"And this," Harshvardhan said, something almost mischievous flickering in his eyes, "is Yug. My youngest. Chief Financial Officer. Also moonlights as a cybersecurity expert for the government, if you can believe that."

Devanshi turned and found herself looking at a man with an infuriatingly put-together face, arms crossed, one eyebrow already raised like he'd decided something about her before she'd even spoken.

"Junior accountant, huh," Yug said, tone flat, almost bored. "Let's see if Dad's instincts are right this time."

Excuse. Me.

"They usually are," she shot back before she could stop herself.

When his father introduced the newly recruited Accounting & Operations Executive, Yug barely spared her more than a glance. Another recommendation, he assumed. 

It wasn't uncommon for people with influential connections to find their way into prestigious companies. But then again... this was Harshvardhan Panchal. His father had rejected relatives for lacking competence; he certainly wouldn't hire someone out of favour. 

That thought alone made Yug look at her properly for the first time. She was petite, almost delicate at first glance, dressed in a simple formal shirt and pencil skirt with her files clutched neatly against her chest. 

There wasn't anything loud or attention-seeking about her. Yet her eyes... they caught him off guard. They were soft enough to reflect innocence, but beneath them burned a quiet fire, the kind that belonged to someone who refused to lose. 

A strange combination. Fragile in appearance. Fierce in spirit. 

He dismissed the observation almost immediately. Looks never impressed him. Competence did. 

And if she truly deserved to stand in Panchal Group, she'd have to prove it like everyone else. Little did he know, she'd soon become the only person capable of challenging him in rooms full of numbers. He glanced at her for the last time, "Hm." 

That was it. Just, **_Hm_**.

He turned back to his laptop, "Welcome, Ms. Patel." Without another word, he walked away. Something flickered in his eyes — surprise, maybe. Or the beginning of a war neither of them had signed up for yet.

Devanshi stared after him, "That's it?"

Harshvardhan rubbed his forehead, "I raised him better than this."

She whispered, "Does he... always talk this much?"

Harshvardhan laughed so hard he almost coughed, "Oh, you have no idea."

Later that afternoon, she finally learnt his name.

Yug Panchal.

▪︎ _Chief Finance Officer.
▪︎ Certified ethical hacker.
▪︎ Cybersecurity consultant to multiple government departments.
▪︎ Occasional coder.
▪︎ Permanent menace._



✎﹏﹏﹏﹏



And a war it became.

Every meeting turned into a quiet battlefield. Devanshi would present her numbers, airtight and confident, and Yug would find the one crack in it... not because he was trying to help, but because apparently his hobby was making her sharp mind stumble.

Twice, he caught errors that made her lose an argument in front of the entire boardroom.

Twice, he wore that infuriating smirk like a crown.

"Cute effort, Ms. Patel," he said once, sliding a corrected report back to her.

"I will end you," she muttered under her breath, loud enough for only him to hear.

"Try," he said, not even looking up.

Meanwhile, her bond with Harshvardhan deepened into something she hadn't expected — he filled a father-shaped hole in her life without either of them ever saying it out loud. 

He'd ask about her day, scold her for skipping lunch, beam with pride every time Raghav mentioned her work.

One afternoon, she entered his cabin carrying reports. He looked up,"Sit."

"I'll stand."

"I said sit."

She smiled, "Yes, Dad."

He shook his head, "You remind me of someone."

"Who?"

"Myself."

She laughed, "Should I take that as a compliment?"

"The biggest one."

Just then, Raghav entered. He watched the interaction quietly. After Devanshi left, he looked at his father.

"You've adopted another child."

Harshvardhan smiled, "I think so."

Raghav chuckled, "Mom will agree too."

Harshvardhan looked toward the closed door, "Some people don't enter your company. They enter your family."

Months in, her portfolio had grown so impressive that Raghav made a call: she'd now head the entire accounting department at the main branch.

Under Yug.

"Great," she'd said dryly, when she found out. "More time with Mr. Sunshine himself."

But underneath the eye-rolls and the bickering, something had already started stirring... quiet, unnamed, and deeply inconvenient.

She stared at the last line, _report to Mr. Yug Panchal,  CFO_, "Seriously?"

From somewhere behind. She heard Yug's voice, "Congratulations, Ms. Patel."

She turned. He stood with his hands in his pockets. Looking annoyingly amused, "Happy?"

"Very much."

He nodded thoughtfully, "Same."

She narrowed her eyes, "So we're both suffering?"

"I suppose." A tiny smile tugged at the corner of his lips, "For the record, I didn't choose you."

She folded her arms, "Good to know. Raghav sir did. I didn't choose you either."

He laughed, "Great. This partnership is already healthy."

She walked away muttering, "I'll resign."

He called after her, "You won't."

She didn't even turn around, "We'll see."

Behind the glass wall of the executive floor, Harshvardhan watched the entire exchange. Beside him stood Raghav, leaning casually against the desk with folded arms.

Harshvardhan smiled to himself, "They argue like Maa and Papa."

Raghav's lips curved into a knowing smirk, "They haven't realized it yet."

Harshvardhan nodded, "No. But I have already."

The circular reached every employee's mailbox at exactly 11:00 a.m. For most people, it was just another internal announcement. For one man...

...it felt like an insult written in bold letters.

**_Internal Circular: Ms. Devanshi Trivedi

"In recognition of her exceptional analytical abilities, operational efficiency, and contribution to the Finance Division, the Management is pleased to appoint her as:

Senior Accounting & Operations Executive, Main Branch

Reporting To: Mr. Yug Panchal, Chief Finance Officer

The Management congratulates her and wishes her continued success.

Issued By: Office of the Managing Director."_**

The office buzzed within minutes.

"_Did you see the mail?"

"She deserves it."

"I heard she solved that investment mismatch Mr. Harshvardhan himself couldn't trace."

"Not only him..." Another corrected with a grin, "...the entire finance team couldn't."

"Imagine getting appreciated by Raghav sir himself."

"I'd frame that email."_

Laughter echoed across the department. Near the window, Devanshi sat frozen in her chair. She had read the email five times. Then once more. Not because she didn't believe it. Because she still couldn't.

One year ago, she had been carrying audit files between cabins.

Today...

The Managing Director himself had signed her promotion letter.

A pair of hands suddenly landed dramatically on her shoulders, "Madam!"

She almost jumped. Her colleague, Sakshi, grinned, "So... When are you giving us a party?"

Another voice joined, "Promotion means Ambika's _Dal-vada_"

"No."

"Pizza."

"Definitely pizza."

Devanshi laughed, "I haven't even joined the new department yet."

"So?"

"Promotion is promotion."

Someone from another desk shouted, "Yug sir is your boss now." A collective silence followed. Then, everyone simultaneously looked at her. 

Devanshi sighed dramatically, "Please don't remind me."

The whole department burst into laughter. Even Devanshi laughed. She leaned back in her chair, "I genuinely don't understand him. He always finds one mistake."

"One. Exactly one. And then he looks so..." She searched for the right word, "...smug."

Another colleague chuckled, "He isn't wrong though."

She narrowed her eyes, "I know. That's what annoys me."

Across the corridor, someone wasn't laughing.

Ramesh Desai.

He stared at the mail on his monitor until the screen dimmed.

Seven years.

Seven years at Panchal Group. Seven years of overtime. Seven years of following instructions. Seven years of hoping someone would notice. And this girl...

One year.
One promotion.
Chairman's favourite.
Managing Director's approval.
The finance department's rising star.

Years at the company, still stuck at junior audit assistant, and watching Devanshi rise faster than him in months had done something ugly to his ego. His jaw clenched. The voices around him grew louder.

"_She's brilliant."

"Have you seen her reports?"

"Mr. Harshvardhan trusts her blindly."

"Even Yug sir challenges her directly in meetings."_

Ramesh slowly closed his laptop. His smile looked calm. His eyes didn't.

Later that day, he'd cornered her about a project, trying to trip her up in front of everyone. She'd dismantled his argument so cleanly that half the office had to hide their laughter. Ramesh's ego didn't just bruise... it shattered.

He decided she needed a "reminder" of her place.

Devanshi looked at everyone, "Any more questions?"

None.

"Great. Let's continue."

Outside the strategy room, a familiar figure stood unnoticed. Hands inside his pockets. Coffee cup in one hand.

Yug Panchal.

He had walked past intending to head to his cabin. Instead...

He had stopped outside after hearing raised voices. He had listened to the entire discussion.

Beside him stood Raghav, "When did you get here?"

"Five minutes ago."

Raghav looked through the glass panel, "So?"

Yug took another sip of coffee, "She came prepared."

"Hm."

Raghav smirked, "You sound impressed."

"I'm not, Bhai."

"Really? Then jealous?" Nisarg teased.

Yug shrugged casually, "Anyone can answer one question."

Raghav raised an eyebrow, "She answered ten before they were asked."

Yug stayed silent. Raghav looked at him knowingly, "You enjoy challenging her."

"I enjoy competent people."

"Hm."

"What's that supposed to mean?"

Raghav's smile widened, "Nothing."

Yug looked back through the glass.

Inside, Devanshi was explaining another chart with complete confidence. He couldn't help noticing how naturally everyone listened to her.

No unnecessary drama.
No loud voice.
Just facts.

She had earned their attention. A tiny smile tugged at the corner of his lips. Barely noticeable.

Raghav caught it anyway.

Behind the conference room door, Ramesh watched Devanshi receiving appreciation from the team. His fists slowly tightened beside him. The humiliation wasn't what hurt the most.

It was the applause. Because every clap she received...

Sounded like another reminder of everything he believed he deserved. And somewhere inside him...

Something ugly quietly took root.

The next morning arrived faster than anyone wanted. By nine-thirty, the executive floor had already slipped into its usual rhythm. Assistants hurried between cabins. Printers worked without pause.

The aroma of fresh coffee drifted through the corridors. And somewhere on the finance floor...

Devanshi was still staring at the same spreadsheet, "You've checked that file four times."

Sakshi peeked over her cubicle.

"If that Yug finds a mistake now, I'm changing professions."

She laughed, "I'll pray for you, Deva."

"Please do."

At the same time, inside the executive conference room.

Raghav stood before the screen, reviewing the day's agenda. Nisarg skimmed through a legal agreement. Harshvardhan quietly read the quarterly summary. Yug walked in last, carrying a laptop under one arm.

Raghav looked up, "Everything ready?"

Yug nodded, "As ready as Finance ever is."

Harshvardhan smiled over his glasses, "And where's our star accountant?"

Yug replied without looking up, "Probably checking numbers for the fifth time."

Harshvardhan chuckled, "I hired the right person."

Yug didn't answer. But the faintest smile crossed his face. Raghav noticed, Nisarg too.

Lunch hour rolled around. The review ended successfully. Employees slowly returned to their departments. Some headed for lunch. Some rushed to finish pending work.

Devanshi stayed behind once again. She wanted to organize tomorrow's files before leaving. The finance floor grew quieter with every passing minute.

She was placing folders into a cabinet when she heard footsteps. She assumed it was Sakshi.

Without looking up, she said, "You forgot your phone again?"

No reply.

She turned. Ramesh stood a few feet away. His expression was unreadable.

"Coffee?" he asked, voice syrupy in a way that made her skin crawl.

"No, thank you," she said, not even looking up.

"Ms. Patel, don't be so uptight —"

"I said no, Ramesh."

That's when he "accidentally" tipped the cup — hot coffee splashing across her fingers and over her feet.

"Aaah!" She jerked back, pain searing up her hand, eyes watering.

Before she could even fully process what happened, Ramesh grabbed both her wrists in one hand, yanking her forward, his other hand clamping hard onto her chin.

"Watch your tone with me," he hissed, fingers digging in.

"Let. Go. Of. Me." Her voice shook from pain, from fury, from the sheer violation of it.
She struggled, twisting against his grip...

CRACK.

A fist collided with Ramesh's jaw so hard he dropped like a puppet with its strings cut.

Yug stood over him, chest heaving, eyes darker than she'd ever seen them.

"Get. Up." His voice was low. Lethal. Ramesh tried to get back up. Yug grabbed him by the front of his shirt.

"You thought..." His voice was frighteningly calm, "...you could touch someone who works under my department?"

Another shove.

"You thought no one would answer for it?" 
When Ramesh didn't move, couldn't move. Yug bent down and hit him again. And again.

"Yug!" Devanshi's voice cracked through her own shock. "Yug, stop, he's already out!"

He didn't stop. Didn't even seem to hear her.

Footsteps thundered in when Raghav, alerted by the commotion, hauling his younger brother back by the shoulders with the practiced strength of someone who'd done this before.

"Yug! Enough." Raghav's voice boomed through the room. "He's unconscious. Stop."

Yug's chest was still heaving, knuckles split and bleeding, staring at the man on the floor like he wanted to do it all over again.

Raghav turned to the security guards hovering by the door, "Keep him in the security holding room until HR and Legal team arrives. File a FIR against him."

"I didn't..."

"Save it." Nisarg had just arrived. His calm voice cut through the room, "You can explain everything in the presence of HR."

"And after that..." He adjusted his glasses, "...you'll explain it to the police."

Ramesh's face lost every trace of colour.

Harshvardhan reached the floor moments later. He wasn't looking at Ramesh. He wasn't looking at the employees. His eyes found Devanshi immediately.

"Beta..." She looked up. The single word was enough. The composure she'd been holding onto cracked, "I'm okay..." She whispered.

Harshvardhan gently placed a hand on her shoulder, "You don't have to be strong every minute." She lowered her head.

Raghav glanced at Yug. His youngest brother's breathing was still uneven. His knuckles were scraped. But his eyes...

They had never left Devanshi, "Yug."

He spoke quietly, "Take her to the pantry. I'll handle everything here."

Yug nodded once.

The guards dragged Ramesh's limp form away. 

He simply walked toward Devanshi, "This way."

For the first time, she followed him without a single sarcastic remark. 

The pantry was silent.

Yug pulled a chair close, kneeling slightly to reach her burned fingers, a small tube of ointment in his hand. His jaw was clenched so tight she thought it might crack. He opened the first-aid box.

"This will sting," he said, voice rougher than usual.

He gently applied burn cream again where yesterday's skin was still irritated, then disinfected the fresh scrape on her wrist. She winced.

"Sorry." It was barely a whisper.

She looked at him, "You've never apologized to me before."

"I wasn't apologizing for correcting your spreadsheets."

Despite everything, a tiny laugh escaped her.

"I noticed you didn't ask before punching a man unconscious," she said weakly, trying and failing to sound unbothered.

"He put his hands on you." Yug didn't look up from her fingers. "I don't apologize for that."

She fell quiet, watching him work... careful, precise, nothing like the sharp-tongued man who lived to argue with her in boardrooms. His thumb brushed gently over the fingerprint bruises on her wrist, and his jaw ticked again, like the sight of it personally offended him.

He finally looked up. His jaw tightened when he noticed the faint finger marks near her wrist. His voice dropped, "Did he hurt you anywhere else?"

She slowly shook her head, "No."

Relief crossed his face so quickly she almost missed it. He wrapped a soft bandage around her wrist with surprising care.

His hands, usually so quick on a keyboard or flipping through financial reports, moved slowly, as though afraid of causing her even the slightest discomfort.

When he finished, he stood, "I'll ask my assistant to arrange for someone to drop you home."

She caught his sleeve before he could leave. He stopped.

"I thought..." She hesitated, "...I thought you couldn't stand me."

For a long moment, neither of them spoke.

Finally, Yug met her eyes, "I still think you argue too much." She almost smiled.

"And your presentations still have too many colours." She laughed softly through watery eyes.

"But listen carefully, Ms. Patel." His expression turned serious again, "You being impossible..."

He paused.

"...doesn't give anyone the right to make you feel unsafe." Silence settled between them.

"Yug?"

"Hm."

"Thank you."

He finally looked up, and for the first time since she'd met him, there was no smirk. No sarcasm. Just something unguarded, something that made her traitorous heart stutter embarrassingly hard.

"Don't get used to it," he muttered, going back to the ointment. "I still think your Q3 report had errors."

Despite everything... the pain, the shock, the sheer chaos of the last twenty minutes, Devanshi laughed. Small, disbelieving, but real.

And for the first time, her cold, guarded heart didn't fight it. It just... softened. Fluttered, even, for the one man she swore she'd never let close.



✎﹏﹏﹏﹏



Outside the pantry's glass wall, Harshvardhan stood quietly. Beside him, Raghav folded his arms.

Harshvardhan watched Yug walk out only after making sure Devanshi was alright. A warm smile spread across his face. Without taking his eyes off his youngest son, he murmured,

"I told you she belonged here."

Raghav's lips curved into a knowing smirk, "You weren't talking about the company, were you?"

Harshvardhan smiled, "No. I was talking about our family."

Raghav smiled, arms crossed, eyes still on the pantry below, "You're not wrong, Dad." A pause, his smirk deepening. "Looks like Yug found his match too."

Harshvardhan chuckled, shaking his head, "Let's see how long that boy lasts pretending he doesn't know it yet."

And somewhere down the corridor, completely unaware of the two men watching him...

Yug Panchal was still convinced he had only protected an employee.

Everyone else already knew...

His heart had made that decision long before his mind ever would.
